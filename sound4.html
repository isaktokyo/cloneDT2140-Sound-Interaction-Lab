    • Interaction 4: Tilt the phone → Ocean waves (and fish)

    What: The phone becomes a small tank of sea water. Tilt it left or right and the water rushes across and breaks against that side as a wave, heard from that side in stereo. Gentle rocking gives small laps; a hard, fast tip gives a big crash with spray on screen. Four fish swim in the tank and make little bubble sounds. Hard crashes hurt them (they flinch and squeak), and too many kill them: a sad descending sound, and they float belly-up. Small laps are harmless, and hurt fish slowly recover. Hold the phone calm for about 4 seconds and a new fish swims in to replace a dead one, with a cheerful plink.

    Why: Tilting a container of water is something everyone has done, so the gesture explains itself: the water behaves like real water, with weight, speed and a bounce off the walls. Rocking in rhythm builds bigger and bigger waves, which makes it playful. The fish add something to care about: the same gesture can be playful or destructive, so you learn to control your force through its consequences. Use case: a calming toy, or a sound layer for a beach or boat scene in a game. The sound is synthesised in the browser from filtered noise (the roar of the breaking wave, the fizz of foam and the thump of water hitting the wall).

    How to use: Tap Turn On DSP, allow motion access, hold the phone flat in front of you, then tip it left and right. Rock it back and forth to build up the waves. The “turned” line shows your tilt and the “shaken” line counts the waves, and the “moved” line shows how many fish are alive. On a laptop, hold the mouse down and drag left and right.
